# Sīla - The Fruits of Virtue

The attainments of Sīla are not a summit reached but a chain set in motion. Ethical conduct does not sit still once established; it produces a definite sequence of mental results that carry the practitioner into concentration and, beyond that, into insight. Unlike the jhānas of Samādhi or the sixteen knowledges of Paññā, the fruits of Sīla are not a graded ladder of states. They are consequences, and the practitioner's task is only to keep the conduct that generates them.

---

## The Three Markers

Sīla taking hold shows up first as three verifiable changes. They are the same markers named in **[RightAction.md](RightAction.md)** and **[RightLivelihood.md](RightLivelihood.md)**, and they are the opening links of the longer chain below.

**Absence of Remorse** (_avippaṭisāra_) - The mind is free of the internal split caused by moral failure. There is no background static of guilt, no preoccupation with past speech or action that resurfaces during meditation. The mind is clean the way a cleared workspace is clean, ready for the next task.

**Social Concord** - Consistent, honest conduct creates trust. The practitioner's relationships lose their hidden agendas, unexploded conflicts, and the residue of broken integrity. This is a structural support for practice, not a soft benefit: a life embedded in healthy relationships generates fewer disturbances to manage.

**Stabilization of Attention** - A mind not producing new remorse has nothing pulling it backward, and a mind not chasing new stimulation has nothing pulling it outward. The field for concentration is clear.

---

## The Fruit-Chain

The _Kimattha Sutta_ (AN 11.1) describes these fruits as a natural cascade. Each stage produces the next without being willed. For a person of virtue, there is no need to form the intention "may non-remorse arise in me"; it arises on its own, and so does everything that follows from it.

| Stage                    | Pali                   | What Arises                                      |
| :----------------------- | :--------------------- | :----------------------------------------------- |
| **Virtue**               | Sīla                   | Conduct kept unbroken                            |
| **Non-remorse**          | Avippaṭisāra           | Freedom from guilt and self-reproach             |
| **Gladness**             | Pāmojja                | A clean joy independent of circumstance          |
| **Rapture**              | Pīti                   | Uplift and energy in body and mind               |
| **Tranquility**          | Passaddhi              | The settling of mental and physical restlessness |
| **Happiness**            | Sukha                  | A stable, contented ease                         |
| **Concentration**        | Samādhi                | The unified, one-pointed mind                    |
| **Knowledge and vision** | Yathābhūta-ñāṇadassana | Seeing things as they actually are               |

The chain shows why Sīla is the base of the whole path rather than a preliminary to be cleared and forgotten. Concentration is not reached by effort alone; it grows out of the gladness and tranquility that unbroken conduct produces. The sequence does not stop at knowledge and vision either. In the sutta it continues through disenchantment and dispassion to liberation, which is the territory of the wisdom training in **[Panna.md](Panna.md)**.

## Purity of Virtue

The commentarial tradition maps the entire path as **seven purifications** (_satta-visuddhi_), and Sīla holds the first place.

1. **Purity of virtue** (_sīla-visuddhi_)
2. Purity of mind (_citta-visuddhi_) - the work of Samādhi
3. Purity of view (_diṭṭhi-visuddhi_)
4. Purity by overcoming doubt (_kaṅkhāvitaraṇa-visuddhi_)
5. Purity by knowledge of path and not-path (_maggāmagga-ñāṇadassana-visuddhi_)
6. Purity by knowledge of the way (_paṭipadā-ñāṇadassana-visuddhi_)
7. Purity by knowledge and vision (_ñāṇadassana-visuddhi_)

The _Rathavinīta Sutta_ frames these as a relay of chariots. A king travels by mounting the first chariot, riding it to the second, changing over, and so on until he arrives. No single chariot completes the journey, and none can be skipped. Purity of virtue is the first chariot. It carries the practitioner only as far as purity of mind, but without it the relay never starts.

## The Grades of Undertaking

The scope of ethical undertaking widens as practice deepens. Each grade is a broader field of the same discipline, not a different one.

- **Five Precepts** (_pañca-sīla_) - The lay baseline: refraining from killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, false speech, and intoxicants.
- **Eight Precepts** (_aṭṭhaṅga-sīla_) - Taken by lay practitioners on observance (_uposatha_) days, adding restraint around food, entertainment, adornment, and luxurious beds to sharpen renunciation.
- **Ten Precepts** (_dasa-sīla_) - The undertaking of novices, extending the eight into a full-time renunciant form.
- **Pātimokkha restraint** - The complete monastic discipline, in which conduct is governed in fine detail so that virtually no occasion for a defilement to express itself is left unexamined.

For the monastic, the tradition also specifies a **fourfold purity of virtue** (_catupārisuddhi-sīla_): restraint under the Pātimokkha, restraint of the senses, purity of livelihood, and the use of requisites with reflection. Each grade removes more of the coarse ground on which defilements act, leaving a mind progressively easier to concentrate.

---

## The Five Benefits

The _Mahāparinibbāna Sutta_ lists five plain benefits that follow from established virtue, spanning this life and beyond it:

1. Wealth accumulated through diligence
2. A good reputation
3. Confidence and composure in any company
4. An unconfused death
5. A fortunate rebirth

These are worldly fruits, and the tradition treats them as real without treating them as the goal. They are what a well-ordered life produces along the way; the point of the training remains the chain that ends in liberation.
